Insights · Healthcare in 2026

The world needs healthcare professionals. Here's where you fit.

Health systems everywhere are under pressure from ageing populations, chronic disease and retiring staff. For skilled Ghanaian professionals, that creates real opportunity — and rewards those who arrive best prepared.

1. The global picture

The World Health Organization's 2025 State of the World's Nursing report found the global nursing workforce grew to nearly 30 million by 2023. Even so, the WHO still projects a shortfall of about 4.1 million nurses — and roughly 11 million health and care workers overall — by 2030. High-income countries such as the UK face ageing workforces and waves of retirement, which keeps demand for qualified professionals strong.

2. The UK today

The NHS is one of the world's largest employers. In June 2026, NHS England reported a registered-nursing vacancy rate of 5.3%, or just over 23,000 open posts, alongside around 100,000 vacancies across its whole workforce. The Royal College of Nursing notes that official vacancy figures understate the true gap because they exclude frozen and unfunded posts.

The market has also become more competitive. Vacancy rates have roughly halved since 2023, and international joiners to the nursing register fell sharply in 2025–26. In practice, that means employers can be selective — and a complete, well-presented dossier is now what separates candidates.

3. Ghana's place in UK healthcare

Ghanaian professionals are already a major part of the UK workforce. In 2025–26, Ghana was the third-largest source of internationally educated nurses and midwives joining the NMC register, after India and Nigeria, with 1,391 new registrants.

Under the UK's ethical recruitment code, Ghana is a country UK employers and agencies may not actively recruit from. That code does not stop Ghanaian professionals from applying to UK employers themselves. Our role is to help you make that application as strong as it can be — documents, registration, English, interviews and family logistics.

4. The UK standards you'll meet

  • Professional registration. Every clinical role is regulated: the NMC for nurses, the GPhC for pharmacy professionals, and the HCPC for radiographers and biomedical scientists. Registration usually comes before, or alongside, your job offer.
  • English language. Regulators set their own thresholds, commonly met with IELTS Academic or OET. Since January 2026, new Skilled Worker applicants must also show English at B2 level.
  • Sponsorship. You need a job offer from a Home Office–licensed UK employer, who assigns your Certificate of Sponsorship (CoS) for the Health and Care Worker visa.
  • Visa benefits. The Health and Care Worker visa has reduced fees, and holders and their eligible dependants are exempt from the Immigration Health Surcharge.
  • Health & character checks. A TB test from a UK-approved clinic and a police clearance certificate are required for you and your travelling family.
